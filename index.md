## Quarter 2 Project Reflections

**1. What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**

I think the most interesting topic covered in my domain this quarter so far has been the intersection of real-world problems and software design. I have been interested in learning how field technicians do their jobs and how the tools they use affect their work. It makes the project more interesting to think about how something we build could actually help someone with a problem they regularly run into.

**2. Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**

I would like to investigate how field technicians interact with their current suite of tools and software, particularly when they need to find information while working on a task. I would like to understand what works well for them and what takes more time or effort than it should. From there, we could choose one issue to focus on and build a small prototype to address it. If we can get access to technicians and the relevant materials, we could test whether our prototype helps them complete that task faster or with fewer mistakes.

**3. What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**

I think that the decision to split the group into three teams has been helpful for dividing responsibilities, but I would like us to have more opportunities to work across those teams. Having more regular check-ins and clearer weekly goals could help everyone understand what the other teams are doing and where they could use support. I think this would also make it easier to bring our work together as we move toward a final result.

**4. What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**

So far, we have done very limited coding, and most of it has been done by one team. I would be interested in getting more involved in building and testing prototypes. It would be useful to take what we have learned about what the technicians actually needs and turn it into something they could try out. Watching how someone uses a prototype and hearing their feedback could help us catch problems we might overlook when discussing ideas as a group.
